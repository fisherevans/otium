Otium · Afterglow · Familiar sessions

An instrument for a little time.

One article in front of you. The surrounding app waits until you need it.

This round takes the selected Afterglow direction into typography and the surrounding experiences. The bottom bar keeps its visual identity while changing its job. In a session, it holds article options, a calm description of time, and Next. In the reader, it helps you return. In the Library, it helps you shape what comes next time.

This is a design prototype. It does not alter the live Otium app or connect to account data. Account-like changes are stored only in this browser.

Your usual session, one tap away.

Open the launcher. Your default leads, then saved presets and a recent unsaved setup. The study starts with ten minutes of news and forty minutes of video. Either goes straight into a session. All opens the full saved or recent list; the launcher keeps a small number visible so it does not grow into a scrolling dashboard.

Custom puts time, section and media type on one screen, then Begin. Other durations and topic exclusions are available in optional sheets. Starting another setup is an explicit choice; editing a draft never changes the active session.

Recent setups remain reusable without being saved. After the same unsaved setup starts twice in a row, a quiet prompt offers to save it at the ending or next start. Any unsaved session also has a save option when it ends. Name it, make it the default, or leave it in Recent. Presets can be edited or removed. Extensions stay part of the original session type rather than creating a five-minute preset by accident.

The walkthrough uses a small deterministic sample and moves time ahead with each story. Forty minutes of video selects the illustrative clip; it does not supply forty minutes of real YouTube content. Presets and recents stay in this browser.

Newsreader · Reading

Something worth reading. Some time of your own.

Brief headlines begin at 28px, or 26px on narrow screens. They can reduce to 24px and use three or four lines, with the full title in the reader. Teasers stay at 16-17px. Photographs retain their complete contents within the available frame; portrait images get letterboxing instead of a crop.

Tap to read. Hold to look closer.

The whole non-video brief opens the story. Hold its image for the full-image viewer, or choose View full image from article options. The reader also has an explicit Full image button. Dragging cancels the tap, and holding never also opens the reader.

A deliberate upward swipe beginning at the bottom of a session article continues to the next brief. Scrolling down to the bottom does not advance it. Back and a rightward swipe return to the current brief. The bottom continuation cue is also a button. Saved articles never advance the session queue.

One instrument, three roles.

Reading has no destination tabs, article fractions, progress meter or ticking clock. Precise duration belongs in setup and session details. The instrument uses a few natural-language phases: Time to settle in, A little time left, Room for one more, and A good place to stop. Time running out never closes the article you are reading.

Swiping or Next advances a single brief. At the end, the session offers a stopping point. Continuing requires an explicit choice of five or ten more minutes. The prototype then repeats its finite fixture set; production would request additional session items.

The instrument stays visible in Settings, History and every other page. Its folded O mark and position remain fixed; session, article and app roles have different labels, shapes and tones. A short content transition makes the change legible. Reduced-motion and e-ink settings skip those transitions.

The rest of Otium uses a clear page title, Back, an app menu and contextual actions. Leaving the session keeps your place. A visible return takes you back to it.

Explore the surrounding experiences.

  • Setup: start a saved or recent session in one tap, or choose time, scope and media on one screen. Matching stories form a finite mock session; empty choices offer a clear way back.
  • Reader: the full title, image, reading type, Save, Share and a return to the originating screen. Publisher links open the actual article.
  • Library: sections, topics, sources, add/import entry points and a clear path back to the session.
  • Source controls: representation, archive eligibility, categories and keywords, with local preference changes.
  • Saved: multiple collection membership, creating and renaming collections, removal and sorting.
  • History and Insights: local preview activity and descriptive composition, without a score or catch-up burden.
  • Appearance: rendered typography, reading size and a light e-ink presentation with grayscale media and immediate changes.

Media with room around it.

The illustrative film is inset, starts paused, and uses the player's own controls. Leaving the brief or opening a sheet pauses it. Its preview menu also lets you inspect portrait framing. The asset is a ten-second motion treatment of a landscape photograph, not a connected YouTube video.

Production YouTube, Shorts and audio remain in scope. The prototype does not claim to validate their platform integrations, autoplay permissions, signed-in watch history or extraction behavior.

What this study does not decide.

It establishes a visual and interaction direction. Production still needs the shared card/reader implementation, full account and source workflows, extraction and retry states, media integrations, history pagination and the existing appearance controls. Structural source/topic edits here are labelled simulations that preserve the fixed comparison set.

Current browser checks cover Firefox, Chromium and WebKit, multiple phone sizes, the selected pairing, the long headline, image framing, reader gestures and core local interactions. Firefox here is the desktop browser engine, not a physical iPhone or an installed PWA. They do not substitute for handling the selected design on a physical iPhone or e-ink device.
