Otium · Afterglow · Second round

An instrument for a little time.

One article in front of you. The surrounding app waits until you need it.

This round takes the selected Afterglow direction into typography and the surrounding experiences. The bottom bar keeps its visual identity while changing its job. In a session, it holds article options, a calm description of time, and Next. In the reader, it helps you return. In the Library, it helps you shape what comes next time.

This is a design prototype. It does not alter the live Otium app or connect to account data. Account-like changes are stored only in this browser.

Start a new session, all the way through.

Open the walkthrough: choose how much time you have, choose a section or topic, optionally leave out specific topics, then Begin. Your choices select matching sample content. Open a story, save it, return, swipe through the session and reach its ending. You can stop, extend, or choose a new session.

The walkthrough moves time ahead as you advance stories, so the natural-language phases and ending can be felt without a ten-minute wait. It uses a small deterministic sample, not the production session engine. Changes to a new setup leave the current session alone until Begin.

Try the type in context.

The arrow strip switches among six real, bundled serif families on the same article. Tap the name or Aa to see the specimens, and choose whether the reading body should match. Close the strip to see the quieter app without the study controls. Your font choice and article position survive a refresh.

Headlines begin at 28px, or 26px on narrow screens. The short-screen fitting pass first gives up teaser lines and image height, then adjusts the title. The longest headline can reach 21px in the narrowest comparison view. Teasers stay at 16-17px. This keeps the comparison grounded in real text lengths without letting the headline consume the entire brief.

Two modes, one visual identity.

Reading has no destination tabs, article fractions, progress meter or ticking clock. Precise duration belongs in setup and session details. The instrument uses a few natural-language phases: Time to settle in, A little time left, Room for one more, and A good place to stop. Time running out never closes the article you are reading.

Swiping or Next advances a single brief. At the end, the session offers a stopping point. Continuing requires an explicit choice of five or ten more minutes. The prototype then repeats its finite fixture set; production would request additional session items.

The rest of Otium uses a clear page title, Back, an app menu and contextual actions. Leaving the session keeps your place. A visible return takes you back to it.

Explore the surrounding experiences.

  • Setup: choose time and scope, then explicitly Begin. Matching stories form a finite mock session; empty choices offer a clear way back.
  • Reader: the full title, image, reading type, Save, Share and a return to the originating screen. Publisher links open the actual article.
  • Library: sections, topics, sources, add/import entry points and a clear path back to the session.
  • Source controls: representation, archive eligibility, categories and keywords, with local preference changes.
  • Saved: multiple collection membership, creating and renaming collections, removal and sorting.
  • History and Insights: local preview activity and descriptive composition, without a score or catch-up burden.
  • Appearance: rendered typography, reading size and a light e-ink presentation with grayscale media and immediate changes.

Media with room around it.

The illustrative film is inset, starts paused, and uses the player's own controls. Leaving the brief or opening a sheet pauses it. Its preview menu also lets you inspect portrait framing. The asset is a ten-second motion treatment of a landscape photograph, not a connected YouTube video.

Production YouTube, Shorts and audio remain in scope. The prototype does not claim to validate their platform integrations, autoplay permissions, signed-in watch history or extraction behavior.

What this study does not decide.

It establishes a visual and interaction direction. Production still needs the shared card/reader implementation, full account and source workflows, extraction and retry states, media integrations, history pagination and the existing appearance controls. Structural source/topic edits here are labelled simulations that preserve the fixed comparison set.

Browser checks cover Chromium and WebKit, multiple phone sizes, six fonts, the long headline and core local interactions. They do not substitute for handling the selected design on a physical iPhone or e-ink device. See validation details.
